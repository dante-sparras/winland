# Tile motion is decided in Core, one step at a time

How a tile moves is decided in Core, where it is tested: whether it Glides, lands at once, or holds, how a Carry and a Super and right-button resize follow the pointer, when a raised window stays above the Bar, how fast the tile clock runs, and whether the tiles have landed. The small decisions those are built from (`TileReturn`, `TileCarry`, `TileHit`) are tested on their own, and how they are put together is where a placer in Platform kept changing.

`TileMotion` in Core makes those decisions. The Win32 placer hands it what it knows: the tiles, the time, the pointer, the buttons, Super, and a way to read a window's rectangle. It gets back the moves, the clock's pace, and a gesture when one finished, and it performs them. The placer keeps the timer, the lock, the four threads that reach it, and everything that is a fact about Windows: the invisible border, the frame, the content bridge, the deferred batch, and the elevated broker. One thread calls `TileMotion` at a time, under the placer's lock. The raise over the Bar and the overflow gate are decided under that lock. The calls that move a window run outside it.

`TileMotion` speaks in the visible frame's pixels, the measure a tile and a window minimum already use. The placer takes the border off a rectangle it reports and adds it to a move.

A step reads a window's rectangle through a lookup, and only for the windows it needs. Reading every tile before each step would add a `GetWindowRect` and two DWM calls per tile to every tick while a mouse button is held. An arrange reads every shown tile, so one that was knocked off its tile is put back. A tick reads only the tiles that are on their way, and reads them all on the sample after a button comes up and while a window waits to hide. While only Super is held the clock runs to see the right button, and a tick reads no window.

A port was the other choice: `TileMotion` would implement `ITilePlacement` itself and call a narrow interface to read and move windows, with an in-memory adapter in tests. That would test `TileFit`'s minimum-size loop against the real overflow gate. It was turned down because Core would take on the lock and the threads, and the in-memory adapter would be a simulation of Windows that nothing checks. `ITilePlacement` stays as it is, and `TileFit` still meets a fake `Overflows` at that seam.
